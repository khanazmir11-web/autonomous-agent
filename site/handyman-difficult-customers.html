Difficult customers: spot them early, keep disputes small

Most customers are fine. A few take far more time than they pay for. You can't avoid every one, but a handful of habits keep a bad job from becoming a bad month.

1. Watch for early warning signs

  • Haggling hard over the quote before any work starts.
  • Vague scope ("just have a look and sort it") with no willingness to agree what "done" means.
  • Bad-mouthing the last tradesperson without any detail.
  • Reluctance to pay a deposit.

One sign is not a verdict. Several together are a reason to price higher or politely decline.

2. Agree scope in writing before you start

Most disputes are two people remembering a conversation differently. A short written scope from your site visit, with price and what is not included, settles most arguments before they begin.

3. Handle changes the same way every time

"While you're here..." is fine when it is written down and priced. See extra work and change orders.

4. Stay calm and keep it in writing

When someone is angry, move the conversation to text or email where you can, and reply after a pause. Stick to facts: what was agreed, what was done, what is left. Keep your tone polite even if theirs isn't. Your photos and job notes do the arguing for you.

5. Offer a fair way forward

Where something is genuinely your fault, put it right (see callbacks and guarantees). Where it is not, explain once, calmly, and offer a quoted price for the extra work. Don't keep doing free work to buy peace; it teaches the customer that pushing works.

6. Know when to walk away

It is acceptable to finish the agreed work, send the invoice and decline future jobs. If safety is at risk, leave. If money owed becomes a real dispute, check the rules where you live, and consider professional advice rather than relying on a blog post, including this one.

7. Price for the awkward jobs

A small buffer for time spent on messages, revisits and waiting is realistic. See how to price a job.
