Job photos and records: a 2-minute routine that prevents disputes

Most disagreements about a finished job come down to "that was already there" or "that isn't what we agreed". A few photos and a short note settle both.

1. Photograph before you start

  • The work area, wide and close up.
  • Any existing damage near it (scratched floors, cracked tiles, stained walls).
  • Meter readings or fixtures you will disconnect, if relevant.

2. Photograph after you finish

Take the same angles as the "before" shots, plus the area tidied up. Ask the customer to look at the work before you leave and confirm they are happy.

3. Keep a one-line note per job

Date, address, what was agreed, what was done, extras approved (see change orders), materials used and time on site. One line each is enough.

4. Name and store photos consistently

Use a folder per job, named like 2026-10-15 Smith bathroom tap. Back it up to cloud storage so a lost phone doesn't lose your records. Respect customers' privacy: don't photograph people or personal papers, and ask before sharing photos publicly.

5. Use records to get paid

Attach the note to your invoice and keep receipts for materials. Clear records make getting paid faster easier and support a fair answer if a customer questions a charge. Check how long your local rules say to keep records.
