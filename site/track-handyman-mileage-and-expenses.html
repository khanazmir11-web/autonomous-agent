How to track mileage and expenses as a handyman

Most solo handymen know what they invoiced last month. Fewer know what they actually kept. The gap is usually small costs that never got written down: fuel, hardware-store runs, consumables and tool replacements. A five-minute routine closes it.

What to record

Mileage

For every job trip, note the date, start and end odometer (or the distance from a map app), the job or customer and the purpose (quote visit, job, supply run). Many tax authorities want a contemporaneous log, so write it the same day rather than reconstructing it in December.

Expenses

For each purchase, note the date, vendor, amount, category and which job it was for (or "general" if it was stock). Useful categories:

  • Materials billed to a customer
  • Consumables (screws, sealant, sandpaper, blades)
  • Tools and equipment
  • Vehicle (fuel, repairs, insurance, if you aren't using a per-mile method)
  • Insurance, licenses, software and phone

A five-minute routine

  1. At the till: photograph the receipt before it leaves your hand. Thermal paper fades.
  2. End of each day: log the day's trips and receipts. Two minutes.
  3. End of each week: match every receipt to a job, then check that billed materials appear on that job's invoice.
  4. End of each month: total the categories and compare against income.

Why match expenses to jobs?

If a job brought in $237 but you spent $102 on materials, drove 18 miles each way twice and bought a $14 blade, you can see whether the job was worth taking. Tracking per job shows which kinds of work pay and which ones quietly lose money, which feeds straight back into how you price the next quote.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing personal and business purchases on one card. Use a separate card or account if you can.
  • Logging only big items. Dozens of $8 purchases add up to hundreds per month.
  • Forgetting unbilled trips, like quote visits that didn't turn into jobs. They are a real cost of winning work.
  • Keeping receipts but no totals. A shoebox is storage, not tracking.
