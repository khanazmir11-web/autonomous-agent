Callbacks and guarantees: handling "it's not working again"

Every handyman gets callbacks. How you handle them decides whether the customer leaves a complaint or a recommendation.

1. State your guarantee in writing

Put one plain sentence on every quote and invoice, for example: "Workmanship is guaranteed for 30 days; materials carry the manufacturer's warranty." Pick a period you can honestly stand behind, and check whether your local rules require anything specific.

2. Say what is not covered

  • Problems caused by something other than your work (a different leak, worn old pipework).
  • Damage from misuse or later changes by someone else.
  • Items the customer supplied, unless you agreed otherwise.

3. Respond fast and listen first

Reply the same day, ask for a photo or short video, and let the customer explain before you defend yourself. Many callbacks are a misunderstanding that a quick message fixes. See the message templates.

4. Check your records

Your before and after photos and job note show what was done and what was already there. Stay polite even when the record is on your side.

5. Fix your own mistakes without fuss

If it is your error, fix it at no charge, promptly, and don't make the customer chase you. If it is outside your guarantee, explain why and offer a fair quoted price to put it right.

6. Log it and learn

Record each callback: job, cause, time spent, cost. If the same cause repeats (a fixing, a product, a rushed step), change your process. Callback time is unpaid, so also allow a small buffer when you price jobs.
