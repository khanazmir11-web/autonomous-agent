A 20-minute month-end money check for handymen

Busy months can feel profitable and still leave you short. Once a month, set aside twenty minutes to look at the real numbers. It is the cheapest way to catch problems early.

1. Chase what you are owed

List every invoice that is unpaid. Send a polite reminder for anything past its due date. Our guide to getting paid faster has wording you can reuse.

2. Add up money in

Total what customers actually paid this month, separate from what you invoiced. Both numbers matter, but only the first one pays your bills.

3. Add up money out

Materials, fuel, tools, phone, insurance, software. Match your receipts and mileage log against your bank statement so nothing is missing. Photograph or file any loose receipts now, while you remember what they were for.

4. Work out what you kept

Money in minus money out is your rough profit before tax. Divide by the hours you worked, including driving, quoting and paperwork. If the hourly figure is lower than you expected, look at your pricing and your supplier runs first.

5. Put money aside for tax

Move a share of this month's profit into a separate account so tax bills don't surprise you. The right share depends on where you live and your situation; ask an accountant or your tax authority rather than copying a number from a blog.

6. Pick one change for next month

One only: raise a price, drop an unprofitable job type, ask for deposits (see deposits and cancellations), or book more repeat work. Write it down and check it next month.
