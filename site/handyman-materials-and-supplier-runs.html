Materials and supplier runs: stop losing hours at the hardware store

For a solo handyman, every trip to the supplier is unpaid time and fuel. A few habits cut the number of trips and make sure the materials you buy actually get paid for.

1. Make the list at the site visit

Write the materials list while you are standing in front of the job, not later from memory. Include fixings, sealant, filler, sandpaper, blades and dust sheets. Small consumables are the usual reason for a second trip. See the site-visit checklist.

2. Buy a little extra of cheap items

Spare screws, wall plugs and a second tube of sealant cost little. A return trip costs far more. Keep leftovers in labelled boxes in the van for the next job.

3. Batch your runs

  • Combine the shopping for tomorrow's and the next day's jobs in one trip.
  • Phone or order online for pickup so you don't browse aisles.
  • Go at quiet times (early morning) rather than the lunchtime queue.

4. Keep every receipt and tag it to a job

Photograph the receipt and note the job it belongs to. Unassigned receipts are either swallowed margin or a headache at tax time. Our mileage and expenses routine covers this in five minutes a day.

5. Charge for materials and the trip

Materials cost you the purchase price plus the time and fuel to collect them. Many handymen add a modest markup on materials and a trip fee, and say so in the written quote so nothing surprises the customer. The pricing guide shows the arithmetic, and a clear quote lists what is included.

6. Decide who buys what, up front

For expensive or choice-driven items (a tap, tiles, a door), agree in writing whether the customer supplies them or you do. If a customer's own materials turn out wrong or faulty, a change of plan is a change order, not a gift.

7. Return what you don't use

Check the supplier's return policy before buying large quantities. Credit unused full packs back to the job or your own stock, and make sure the customer isn't billed for materials they didn't receive.
