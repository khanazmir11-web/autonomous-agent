Handling extra work and change orders

"While you're here, could you also…?" is one of the most common sentences in home repair. Said yes to every time, it quietly eats your profit. Refused every time, it costs you goodwill. The fix is a short, repeatable routine so extra work is always agreed and priced before you start it.

1. Know the two kinds of extra work

  • Customer-requested additions: "Can you also fix the gate?"
  • Surprises you find: rotten wood behind the tile, an old pipe that won't take the new fitting, a wall that isn't what the customer thought.

Both need the same treatment: stop, describe, price, agree, then continue.

2. Say it in the original quote

Put one line in your quote terms: "Work outside the described scope, or problems found once work begins, are quoted separately and agreed before they start." It turns a later conversation into something you both already expected. See writing a quote that wins for scope and exclusions.

3. The five-step routine

  1. Pause. Don't begin the extra task, even a "five minute" one.
  2. Describe. One or two plain sentences: what it is and why it's extra.
  3. Price it. Use the same method as any job: labor hours, materials with markup, and the minimum charge if it applies (the pricing method). Don't discount on the spot because you feel awkward.
  4. Get a yes in writing. A text or email reply is enough for most small jobs: "Reply YES to approve the extra work at $X."
  5. Record it. Add it to the job so the final invoice matches what was agreed.

4. A simple change-order wording

Copy and adapt:

"Hi [name], while working on [job] I found [issue / you asked about X]. This is outside the original quote. To do it I'd need about [time] and [materials]. The extra cost would be $[amount], bringing the total to $[new total]. If you'd like me to go ahead, just reply YES. If not, I'll finish the original job as quoted."

More wording is in the customer message templates.

5. Handle the awkward cases

  • Tiny favours. It's your call to do a two-minute favour free. Mention it ("no charge this time") so it doesn't become the expectation.
  • Safety problems. If you find something unsafe (wiring, gas, structure) outside your skills or licence, say so and recommend a qualified professional.
  • The customer says no. Finish the original scope, and note what you advised. Don't do work you aren't comfortable with just to avoid friction.
  • Big changes. If the extra work changes the timeline or needs a new deposit, treat it as a new quote.

6. Invoice what was agreed

Show the original quote and each approved change as separate lines, with the dates they were approved. Customers rarely dispute an invoice that matches messages they sent. See getting paid faster for deposits and follow-up.
