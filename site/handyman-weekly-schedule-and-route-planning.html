Weekly schedule and route planning for a solo handyman

Driving is unpaid time. A schedule that groups jobs by area and leaves room for overruns gives you more billable hours without working longer days.

1. Plan the week once, on the same day

Pick a regular time (Friday afternoon or Sunday evening) to lay out next week. List every confirmed job with its address, estimated duration and any materials to collect.

2. Group jobs by area

  • Assign each area of your patch a day or half-day where you can.
  • Offer customers a choice of two slots inside the day you're already in their area.
  • Charge a trip fee that reflects far-away jobs.

3. Put supplier runs at the edges of the day

Collect materials on the way to the first job or on the way home, not in the middle. See materials and supplier runs.

4. Add buffers

Jobs overrun. Add about 20% to each estimate, and leave one open slot a day for small callbacks and emergencies. Do your site visits in blocks rather than scattered.

5. Confirm the day before

Send a short message with arrival window and what you'll need access to (templates in customer message templates). It cuts no-shows; see deposits and cancellations.

6. Review monthly

Compare miles driven with hours billed. If one area eats your week for little revenue, raise prices there or stop serving it. Log mileage as in mileage and expenses.
