A handyman's slow-season plan

Most solo handymen have quiet stretches: after the holidays, in deep winter, or in a rainy spell. Quiet weeks are stressful, but they are also the only time you have to fix the business itself. Here is a simple plan you can run through in a few short sessions.

1. Review what actually happened

Look back over the last 6 to 12 months. For each job type (e.g. fence repair, tiling, small plumbing) note how many quotes you sent, how many you won, the average job value and roughly how long it took. Which jobs paid best per hour? Which ones cost more time than they earned? Do more of the first kind.

2. Plan cash before it gets tight

Add up your fixed costs per month: insurance, phone, van, tool payments, software. That is the amount you need to cover even in a bad month. Aim to keep a cushion of a month or two if you can, and set aside money for tax as you earn it. Ask a qualified accountant what the right amount is where you live; see also tracking mileage and expenses.

3. Check your prices

If you haven't raised prices in a year, costs have probably moved. Recalculate your hourly rate, materials markup, trip fee and minimum charge using the pricing method and the free Quote Calculator. Apply new prices to new quotes; don't change the price of a quote you've already given.

4. Chase what's owed

Quiet weeks are the right time to clear unpaid invoices and stale quotes. Use the routine in getting paid faster and the follow-up wording in the message templates.

5. Ask past customers if they need anything

Reply to people who have already worked with you or asked for a quote: "Hi [name], I have some openings in [month]. If there's anything on your list, I'm happy to quote." Keep it to people who know you, send it once, and respect a "no". Don't mass-message strangers.

6. Fix the small stuff in your business

  • Tidy and restock the van; sharpen, repair or replace tools.
  • Update your quote template and terms (see writing a quote that wins).
  • Check insurance, licences and renewal dates for your area.
  • Take photos of your best finished jobs (with the customer's permission).
  • Ask happy customers once for a short review; never pay for or write one yourself.

7. Pick one thing to improve

Don't do everything. Choose one: raise prices, win more quotes, or get paid faster. Write it down, check it again at the end of the season.
