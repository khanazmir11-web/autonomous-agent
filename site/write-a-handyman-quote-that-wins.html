How to write a handyman quote that wins the job

A good quote does two things: it makes the customer feel safe saying yes, and it protects you from doing unpaid extra work. Price matters, but a clear, prompt, professional quote often beats a slightly cheaper vague one.

1. Describe the scope in the customer's words

Say what you will do, where, and what "done" looks like. "Replace the kitchen tap with the customer-supplied mixer tap, including removing the old one and checking for leaks" is better than "plumbing work". Repeating their request back shows you listened and prevents misunderstandings later.

2. List what is not included

Exclusions are not negative; they are clarity. Examples: "Does not include repairing hidden damage found after the old fixture is removed", "Paint not included", "Rubbish removal not included". Say how extra work will be handled: you will tell the customer and agree the price before doing it.

3. Show the price in a way that is easy to trust

  • For small jobs, one fixed price with labor and materials separated.
  • For uncertain jobs, a range or a rate with an estimated number of hours.
  • Include any trip fee or minimum charge up front rather than surprising them later.

Not sure how to build the number? See how to price a handyman job.

4. Add a start window, validity date and payment terms

"This quote is valid for 14 days. Earliest start: next Thursday. A deposit of the materials cost is due before ordering; the balance is due on completion." Material prices change, so a validity date is fair to you and honest to them. More on terms in getting paid faster.

5. Send it fast and make accepting easy

Quote the same day if you can. Customers often go with the first professional reply. Let them accept by replying "yes" to the message, and keep that reply as your record.

6. Follow up once or twice, politely

  1. 2 to 3 days later: "Just checking you received the quote and whether you have any questions."
  2. About a week later: a short note that the slot is still available until the validity date.

Don't invent urgency. If they say no, thank them and move on. Note the outcome so you can see over time which kinds of quotes you win.

Quick checklist

  • Scope in plain words
  • Exclusions and how extras are handled
  • Price split into labor and materials
  • Validity date and start window
  • Payment terms and accepted methods
  • Your contact details and a simple way to say yes
