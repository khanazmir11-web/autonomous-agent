Van stock and tool checklist for a solo handyman

A well-stocked van turns "I'll have to come back" into "done today". The goal is not to carry everything, only the cheap, fast-moving items that most often cause return trips.

1. Core hand tools

  • Tape measure, spirit level, square, pencil and marker
  • Screwdriver set (including small and precision bits), hex keys, adjustable spanners, pliers, mole grips
  • Hammer, mallet, pry bar, utility knife with spare blades, stud/cable detector
  • Torch or head lamp, and a spare battery or charger

2. Power tools and batteries

  • Cordless drill/driver and impact driver, with two charged batteries each day
  • Drill and driver bit sets, hole saws, masonry bits
  • Oscillating multi-tool, jigsaw or hand saw, sander
  • Extension lead and a way to protect from electric shock (such as an RCD adapter) where your local rules call for it

3. Fixings and consumables

  • Assorted screws, wall plugs/anchors, nails, washers, cable ties
  • Sealant (silicone and decorator's caulk), filler, adhesive, PTFE tape
  • Sandpaper, masking tape, dust sheets, bin bags, cloths
  • Touch-up paint in the colours customers commonly use, if you offer that

4. Safety and protection

  • Gloves, safety glasses, dust masks, ear protection
  • First-aid kit and a fire extinguisher
  • Shoe covers or mats for indoor work; a vacuum for cleaning up
  • Ladder in good condition, checked before use

5. Paperwork pocket

  • Printed or digital quote and change-order forms (see extra work and change orders)
  • Copy of your insurance details, if you carry insurance
  • Receipt folder or phone album for photographing receipts

6. A five-minute weekly restock

Pick one fixed time (Friday afternoon works for many people).

  1. Charge all batteries and put them back.
  2. Count the boxes of screws, plugs and sealant tubes; write down anything below two.
  3. Check consumables you used on this week's jobs, and note them against those jobs so the cost is charged, not absorbed.
  4. Add everything to one supplier run; see materials and supplier runs.
  5. Wipe tools and put them back in the same place. A tool you can't find costs minutes every day.

7. Use the next day's jobs to decide what to pack

Look at tomorrow's list the evening before. Your site-visit notes should already say what each job needs. Pack for those jobs first and fill the rest from the standard kit.

8. Treat tool and van costs as business costs

Keep the receipts for tools, consumables and van running costs and log them with the job or category they belong to. The mileage and expenses routine shows how. What counts as deductible depends on where you live, so check with a qualified accountant.
