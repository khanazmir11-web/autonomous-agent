Deposits, cancellations and no-shows: a simple policy for a solo handyman

A cancelled slot is lost income you can't get back. A short, fair, written policy protects your time without making customers feel distrusted.

1. Decide what a deposit is for

A deposit is usually used for two reasons: to cover materials you must buy up front, and to hold a day in your calendar. Say which in the quote. Many handymen only ask for a deposit on jobs with special-order materials or jobs of several days, and not on small repairs.

2. Pick a simple amount

  • Materials-heavy job: the cost of materials you must pre-buy.
  • Large job: a fixed share of the quote (many people use 10-30%). Choose what suits your trade and area.
  • Small job: no deposit, but a cancellation rule (below).

The pricing guide and the free quote calculator include a deposit line so the amount is written on the quote.

3. Write the cancellation rule in the quote

Put it in plain words before the customer accepts. For example:

  1. Free to reschedule or cancel with at least 24 hours' notice.
  2. Less notice, or no one home when I arrive: a call-out fee of [amount], which is my trip fee.
  3. Deposits cover materials already bought. Unused materials are returned for refund where I can, and the rest is passed on at cost.

Keep the numbers small and honest. Never charge for costs you didn't actually incur.

4. Confirm the day before

Send a short reminder with time, address and anything the customer needs to prepare. See the message templates for wording. Most no-shows are people who forgot.

5. When someone is late or absent

  • Wait a fixed, reasonable time (for example 15 minutes) and message once.
  • Log the visit, time and fuel so the trip fee is traceable; photograph the door if needed.
  • Fill the gap: keep a short list of small jobs and quote-ready leads you can offer same-day.

6. Be fair when life happens

Illness, a burst pipe at their house or a family emergency: waiving the fee once often earns a loyal customer. Make it your choice, not an automatic rule. Repeat offenders get a deposit next time; see reviews and repeat customers.

7. Record it

Note every deposit received, what it covered and any refund or call-out fee on the job record so the numbers match when you invoice. See getting paid faster.
